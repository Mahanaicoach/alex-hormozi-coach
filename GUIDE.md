# Alex Hormozi Coach — Full Guide

How the skill works under the hood, and how to get the most out of a session.

---

## Part 1 — What it actually does

It turns Claude into a business coach that runs the **Alex Hormozi hotline method**. Not generic advice — a real diagnostic conversation: it gets your numbers, distrusts the problem you *think* you have, finds the real constraint, proves it with live math, and prescribes the smallest high-leverage move you can make this week.

It coaches **one question at a time**, like a phone call. The back-and-forth *is* the method. It won't dump a wall of advice — each answer you give sharpens its next question.

## Part 2 — How it works, step by step

**1. It triages you into a stage (always first).**
It opens with energy — *"Rock and roll. Tell me about the business — what do you sell, how much you make?"* — then places you in one of four stages. This matters more than anything: the #1 failure of AI business advice is giving a scaling answer to someone with no customers yet.

| Stage | You are… | It works on… |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | No business / no revenue yet | Your first 5 customers — warm outreach, free offers, proof |
| **1** | Have an offer, shaky revenue | A **Grand Slam Offer** — pricing, guarantees, naming |
| **2** | Running & profitable | The full **diagnostic loop** → money model *(most people land here)* |
| **3** | Profitable but plateaued | **More** — volume, the real constraint, parallel machines |

Stages aren't locked. If your real block lives in another stage mid-session, it re-routes (e.g. a profitable business whose true problem is a broken offer jumps back to Stage 1).

**2. It asks adaptively, not from a script.**
Only the opening question is fixed. Every question after it comes from your last answer. When you name a category ("I run an agency"), it drills into the **sub-type** first — an SEO agency, an SMMA, and an AI-automation agency are completely different businesses with different channels, churn, and money models. It usually gives you pickable options so you can just choose.

**3. It runs the core loop.**
No matter the stage, underneath runs the same five-beat loop:
1. **Distrust the stated problem** — it's a symptom. Pull the thread to the real constraint.
2. **Call the shot** — say the hypothesis out loud *before* proving it.
3. **Prove it with math** — CAC, LTV, margin, 30-day cash, payback — in round numbers, live.
4. **Smallest change first** — the cheapest, most reversible high-leverage move, as "thing one, thing two, thing three."
5. **Close with "what's stopping you?"**

**4. It checks your numbers against benchmarks.**
It carries the healthy ranges in its head, so it instantly knows whether a number is fixable or fine:
- **LTGP : CAC ≥ 3:1** — below that, the *business model* is the problem, not the ads
- **Recover CAC within 30 days** — the single most important money-model test; if you do, scale is unlocked
- **< 2%/month churn** (≥ 80%/year retention) — above ~8%/month is an emergency
- **Speed to lead < 60 seconds** — calling inbound leads within a minute can roughly *double* conversion
- **Inbound close rate 50–60%** — 30% is low and usually liftable
- **"99% of businesses need to raise prices, not lower them"**

**5. It stays in voice.**
Short sentences. Round numbers out loud. Blunt but warm. Conversational prose — never a markdown report with a "Summary" heading. You should feel like you're on the phone with him.

## Part 3 — How to get the most out of it

### Know your numbers before you start (or let it estimate)
The coaching is only as sharp as your inputs. If you can, walk in knowing:
- **Revenue** and **profit** (after paying yourself)
- **Price** and **churn** (what % of customers you keep per year)
- **CAC** — what it costs to get one customer

Don't have them? That's fine — it'll back into estimates with you ("Of 100 customers a year ago, how many are still with you?"). But if you *truly* can't answer anything, that itself becomes the first prescription: **know your numbers**.

### Answer honestly, even when it stings
It finds the real constraint by pulling the thread on your answers. If you inflate your numbers or dodge, it diagnoses the wrong problem. The blunt truth is the value — give it the real picture.

### Let it run one question at a time
Don't paste your whole situation and demand a full plan. Answer the one question, wait, answer the next. If it ever dumps a wall of advice, tell it *"one question at a time"* — the interactive loop is what makes it work.

### Push it for the math
The prescription is undeniable when the numbers prove it. If it gives advice without showing the math, ask: *"show me the math on that."* Make it compute CAC, payback, and 30-day cash live — that's how you'll actually believe the move.

### Ask for the smallest move first
You have a tiny change-budget — any change costs ~20% output dip while you adjust. Don't ask "what's my 5-year plan." Ask: *"what's the smallest, highest-leverage thing I can do this week?"* Then do it, see the result, come back, and layer up.

### Use it to get talked OUT of busywork
One of its best moves is pushing back. If you're:
- Optimizing a 30% opt-in to 35% → it'll redirect you to volume
- Chasing a *new* channel when you should do *more* of the working one → it'll call it
- Solving the same problem twice in a month → it'll tell you to give it 90 days

Bring it your "I'm thinking of trying X" ideas and let it tell you which are distractions.

### Re-run when your numbers change
This isn't one-and-done. After you make the move, come back: *"I raised the price three weeks ago, here's what happened."* New numbers → new constraint → next move. That's how real coaching compounds.

### Great prompts to use

```
coach me — [what you sell], [revenue], [the problem you think you have]
```
```

```
```
I'm booked solid but can't scale. what's the real constraint?
```
```
talk me out of (or into) launching a new offer
```
```
what's the smallest thing I can do this week to grow?
```

## Part 4 — What it won't do (the guardrails)

It's a business coach in a specific style — not everything:
- **No earnings promises.** "Results vary. Most people do nothing." It won't guarantee an income.
- **No legal, tax, or investment advice.** It'll explain how a money model *works*, but won't tell you to take a loan, make an investment, or sign something — that goes to a professional.
- **No medical/health claims** if your business touches that space — it sticks to business mechanics.
- **It's human first in a crisis.** If you're in real financial distress, it drops the operator persona and points you to the lowest-risk free action.
- **It doesn't claim to *be* Alex Hormozi.** It channels his frameworks and style for educational coaching. (See the disclaimer in the README — this is independent, fan-made, and not affiliated with him.)
