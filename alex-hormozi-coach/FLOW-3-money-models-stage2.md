# FLOW 3 — Stage 2: Running & profitable → diagnose + build the money model
### Source: the live coaching loop + $100M Money Models (see COACHING-FRAMEWORK.md, REF-money-models.md)

This is the hotline default — the person who can give you real numbers. Run the **diagnostic loop (3.1)** first; it almost always points at a money-model fix (3.2) or a constraint (hand to FLOW-4). The money-model sub-flows (3.3–3.6) are the prescriptions.

---

## FLOW 3.1 — The diagnostic loop (run on every Stage 2 caller)
**When:** they have revenue and a stated problem.
**Steps:**
1. **Intake — numbers first.** "Tell me about the business. What do you sell, how much you make?" Pull, in order, and restate each in round numbers: **revenue → profit → price (or annual customer value) → churn/retention.** Don't advance without them.
2. **Surface the stated problem.** "What do you want to have happen? What's the problem?" Treat the answer as a symptom.
3. **Pull the thread.** Trace it back: keep asking "why can't you…" until you hit the root lever. (Phil: "can't retain staff" → can't pay → prices low → sales motion broken.)
4. **Call the shot.** Say the hypothesis out loud: "Here's my bet — we fix X first, then Y, then Z."
5. **Prove with unit economics.** Compute live, in round numbers: CAC (not CPL) · LTV / annual gross profit · gross margin · **first-30-day cash** · payback ratio. Let the math make the case.
6. **Pick the reframe** (constraint / feature-not-bug / activation / parallel machine — see FLOW-4 library).
7. **Determine the constraint:** demand-constrained or supply-constrained?
   - **Demand** (not enough customers) → fix money model (3.2) to fund more acquisition, then drive volume (FLOW 1.5–1.9).
   - **Supply** (can't deliver/hire) → build the parallel machine (FLOW 4.4).
8. **Prescribe smallest change first** as "thing one, thing two, thing three."
9. **Close:** "So what's stopping you?" → "Rock and roll. Appreciate you."
**Done when:** the root constraint is named, the math supports the move, and a sequenced prescription is delivered.

---

## FLOW 3.2 — Build / fix the money model
**When:** the diagnostic shows weak cash flow, no offer sequence, or CAC not recovered fast.
**Goal:** "Make more profit from a customer than it costs to get and service them in the **first 30 days**." Best case: enough 30-day profit from one customer to acquire two more.
**Steps:**
1. Measure current **30-day cash per customer** vs. CAC. If you don't recover CAC in 30 days, that's the target.
2. Build in three stages, **one at a time, never all at once**:
   - **Get cash** → add/sharpen an **attraction offer** (3.3).
   - **Get more cash** → add **upsells** (3.4) and **downsells** (3.5).
   - **Get the most cash** → add **continuity** (3.6), last.
3. Re-check: can one customer's 30-day profit now fund the next acquisition? If yes, you've got **client-financed acquisition** — scale is unlocked.
**Rules:** "Simple scales, fancy fails." "We don't get customers to make a sale; we make sales to get customers." Make each offer at the customer's moment of greatest need.
**Done when:** CAC is recovered inside 30 days and offers are sequenced.

---

## FLOW 3.3 — Attraction offer (get cash up front)
**When:** need more/cheaper customers at the front.
**Steps:** pick ONE and build it:
- **Win Your Money Back** — "do X / hit Y in Z days → it's free." Criteria must be trackable, likely, and advertise you. Only if refund rate <5%. Pay winnings as store credit over time.
- **Giveaway** — chance to win the flagship for contact info + actions; one public winner, everyone else "qualifies" for the promo. Discount = 10–30% of margin.
- **Decoy** — advertise a cheap/free simple version; present both, push the premium. Script: "Are you here for free stuff or lasting results?"
- **Buy X Get Y Free** — reframe a discount as free units; give more free than paid; raise price before adding free.
- **Pay Less Now or Pay More Later** — card on file either way; pay-now gets 20–50% off + bonuses; pay-later billed unless they cancel (and must meet criteria to qualify to cancel).
**Done when:** one attraction offer is live and pulling customers at/under target CAC.

---

## FLOW 3.4 — Upsell (get more cash per customer)
**When:** customers buy once but there's an obvious next need.
**Steps:** pick the fit:
- **Classic** — "you can't have X without Y." Solve the next problem the instant they feel it. Offer highest-profit upsell first. Use **BAM FAM** (book a meeting from a meeting) and **magnetic-middle** 3-tier pricing.
- **Menu Upsell (the flagship 4 steps, in order):** ① **Unsell** what they don't need (cross it out — builds trust, especially low-margin items) → ② **Prescribe** what they do need, integrated and personalized, assuming the buy → ③ **AB** ("chocolate or vanilla?" — either is a yes) → ④ **Card on file** ("use the card we have?"). This is the supplement-sale script from the live session.
- **Anchor** — present a premium 5–10× option → get "the gasp" → rescue them → present the real offer (now a deal) → ask how they'll pay. Anchor and main share the same primary features.
- **Rollover** — credit a past purchase toward a bigger next offer. Price the next offer ≥ **4× the credit** (so full credit ≤ 25% off). Target lapsed/upset customers; add one-time urgency.
**Done when:** an upsell is attached at the point of greatest need.

---

## FLOW 3.5 — Downsell (capture the "no")
**When:** prospects say no to the main offer. **Cardinal rule: never offer the same thing for less.**
**Steps:** pick:
- **Payment Plan** (changes *how* they pay, not the price) — 7-step ladder: reward pay-in-full → financing → half now/half next paycheck → temperature check (1–10; 8+ continue, ≤7 pivot) → split in 3 → even spread → free trial. Data: monthly billing churns 10.7%/mo, quarterly 5%, annual 2% — so push fewer, bigger payments.
- **Trial With Penalty** (recurring offers) — offer the trial LAST; always take a card; sell "staying and paying"; explain fees after the card; make check-ins required. (You only pay if you *don't* comply.)
- **Feature Downsell** (changes *what* they get) — remove features highest-value first, drop price, ask "how about now?" Often flips them back to the original once they see what's missing. Alternate payment-plan and feature downsells to become "very difficult to refuse."
**Done when:** every "no" has a different, lower-friction path that protects price integrity.

---

## FLOW 3.6 — Continuity (get the most cash, recurring)
**When:** attraction/upsell/downsell are working; now stack recurring revenue. Add this **last**.
**Steps:** pick:
- **Continuity Bonus** — advertise an awesome bonus (worth more than the first payment), not the membership; they get it free by joining. Price standalone vs. continuity to nudge into recurring (2× standalone → ~70% pick continuity).
- **Continuity Discount** — free time for a commitment; choose front-load (more signups, more churn) vs. back-load (fewer signups, less churn). **Highest-value tip in the book: bill every 4 weeks, not monthly** → 13 cycles = +8.3% revenue. Add a 3% "processing fee" (waivable for ACH).
- **Waved Fee** — setup fee = 3–5× monthly; waive it for a 1-year commit; early cancel pays the fee. "Customers stay longer when leaving costs more than staying."
**Done when:** recurring revenue is stacked on top of the 30-day cash, churn is acceptable (≥80%/yr retention).

---

## Stage 2 graduation
When the model funds its own acquisition and the block becomes "I literally can't make/deliver/hire more" → move to FLOW-4 (More / scale).
