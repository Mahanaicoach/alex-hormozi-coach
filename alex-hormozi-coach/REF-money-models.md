# REF: $100M Money Models (Hormozi) — Coaching Playbook Extraction

**Use when:** client ALREADY has revenue/customers and needs to monetize better, sequence offers, fix cash flow, or lower effective CAC. This book answers "how do I get them to buy?" (Offers = what to sell, Leads = how to find them, Models = how to get them to buy more, faster.)

---

## CORE FRAMEWORK: The Money Model

**Definition (verbatim):** "A money model is a sequence of offers." More precisely: "what you offer, when you offer it, and how you offer it to make as much money as you can as fast as you can."

**The cash-flow goal (the whole point):**
- Bare minimum good money model: "Make more profit from a customer than it costs to get and service them in the first 30 days."
- A **$100M money model**: "makes so much money in the first 30 days that the cost of getting more customers will never be a problem again" — make enough profit from ONE customer to "get and service at least two more customers in less than 30 days."
- **The 30-day rule of thumb:** "I like to cover my cost of getting a customer within 30 days." Reason: any business can get interest-free money for 30 days via a credit card — pay it off before month-end and recycle it. "Rinse and repeat."
- **Growth math:** double customer value → grow 8x faster; triple it → 27x faster (more customers × bigger value × faster payback compound).

**Three stages (build ONE at a time, never all at once):**
1. **Get cash** — attraction offers (get customers for less)
2. **Get more cash** — upsells + downsells (more money, faster, from each)
3. **Get the most cash** — continuity (maximize total spend)

"If you try to start a bootstrap business from zero with a finished money model, it will collapse on top of you." Measure progress in quarters, not weeks. "Simple scales, fancy fails." "It's less about having 100 products to offer and more about having 100 ways to offer your product."

**Rules of thumb across all offers:** Hard selling is for weak products. Make offers at the customer's time of greatest need. "We don't get customers to make a sale. We make sales to get customers." Affiliate products can fill gaps in your model.

---

## THE FOUR OFFER TYPES
Attraction → Upsell → Downsell → Continuity. Any offer can be used alone, in any order. Hormozi uses all four in his best businesses.

---

## 1. ATTRACTION OFFERS (turn strangers into customers, get cash up front)
Free/discount = great deal. "The greatest discount of all being free." Free and discount and $1 are interchangeable. **Five named attraction offers:**

### 1A. Win Your Money Back
- **Terminology:** "If you do X or achieve Y within Z days, you can get it free."
- **Mechanics:** Customer puts money down. Set a goal + show them how to reach it. They bet on (a) results, (b) actions, or (c) both. If they hit it → money back as cash OR store credit (testing showed store credit pulls equal numbers, so use it).
- **Criteria must be:** easy to track, likely to produce the result, and advertise the business (post/tag/refer/review).
- **When to reach for it:** businesses where customers must put in continuous effort (fitness, weight loss, learning skills, starting a business, beauty/self-care). Only use if current refund rate is **below 5%**.
- **Verbatim:** "This offer has generated $1 billion in sales." "The real money comes from people who succeed with it and you have something else to offer them." Apply winnings as store credit spread over a long period (e.g. $600 over 12 months = $50/mo off), NOT free months up front. "Make everyone a winner" privately — mid-program, credit them toward the next thing as if they've already won.

### 1B. Giveaways (scholarship / sweepstakes / raffle)
- **Mechanics:** Advertise a chance to win a big prize for contact info + qualifying actions. Pick a grand prize (= the thing you want everyone to buy, with a $ value anchor). Pick promotional offer (discounted version for everyone else). Deadline 3–7 days. Announce ONE grand-prize winner publicly, then privately tell everyone else they "qualified" for the promotional offer.
- **Rule of thumb:** core-offer discount = 10–30% of gross margins. "If your giveaway doesn't work, it means your grand prize wasn't grand enough."
- **When:** lead generation when you have a high-value flagship product. Give TWO prizes ("if someone you refer wins, you win too") to double referral leads.

### 1C. Decoy Offer
- **Terminology:** "Which one do you think will get you the best results?" / "You need to know what gets results for customers better than they do."
- **Mechanics:** Advertise a free/cheap simpler version (decoy). When leads engage, present BOTH but emphasize the premium. Strip the decoy of guarantees/features; make contrast huge.
- **Key script:** "Are you here for free stuff or lasting results?" If results → skip to premium. Origin: the **$5 five-day VIP tanning pass + "turkey talk."**
- **When:** flexible across any business; great when you know customers' problems better than they do. Discount offers have higher SHOW-UP rates than free offers (use for doctors/dentists/lawyers).

### 1D. Buy X, Get Y Free
- **Terminology:** "Buy one puppy, get two puppies free" (Boot Factory: buy one pair of boots, get two free at 3x price).
- **Mechanics:** Reframe a discount as free stuff. Selling 3 shirts at $10 = "buy one for $30, get two free" — same price, more perceived value. Give MORE free than paid (buy 1 get 2, not buy 2 get 1). Raise prices BEFORE giving free stuff to preserve margin.
- **When:** products/services that make sense to buy more of or get longer access to. Generates big cash up front — "make sure you can deliver for the whole year." Great for a fast cash pop to existing recurring customers (cap at ~10% of base).

### 1E. Pay Less Now or Pay More Later
- **Terminology:** "Time is money." Origin: the "double your reading speed in 3 hours or it's free" offer.
- **Mechanics:** Two options. **Pay later** = $0 now, card on file, conditional satisfaction guarantee, billed later unless they cancel (must meet criteria, e.g. attend, to qualify to cancel). **Pay now** = 20–50% discount + bonuses. You get the card on file either way.
- **When:** digital products + short-duration services where you can deliver a clear yes/no result in the window. Tuning: if >10% of pay-later cancel, you over-promised or the guarantee bar is too low or price too high.

## 2. UPSELL OFFERS ("whatever you offer next")
"When an offer solves a problem, another appears." "Do you want fries with that?" Burger profit example: 25¢ → $3 across upsells. "Your first offer doesn't always have to make a profit." Upsell = more (quantity), better (quality), or new/complimentary (different). **Four named upsells:**

### 2A. The Classic Upsell
- **Terminology:** "You can't have X without Y." (Can't have a car without insurance/gas.)
- **Mechanics:** Solve the customer's NEXT problem the moment they become aware of it. Offer more profitable upsells first. "Get them to say no to say yes": ask "You don't want anything else, do you?" — the no = a yes. Give bonus access ASAP ("it's way harder to give something back once you've gotten it"). Bundle and NAME bundles.
- **BAM FAM** = "Book A Meeting From A Meeting." End every appointment by booking the next. Use the **magnetic middle / three-option pricing** to nudge buyers up a tier.

### 2B. Menu Upsells (THE 4-STEP UPSELL — flagship)
- **Terminology:** "You don't need that. You need this." Origin: the supplement-shelf story.
- **The 4 tactics IN ORDER:**
  1. **Unsell** — tell them what they DON'T need (cross items out → builds goodwill, especially unsell low-margin stuff)
  2. **Prescribe** (prescription upsell) — tell them what they DO need, how it integrates with what they bought, personalized in detail (assume they're buying)
  3. **AB upsell** — ask preference between A or B (not yes/no): "chocolate or vanilla?" Either choice = a sale. Add a nudge.
  4. **Card on file** — "Do you want to use the card you have on file?" (removes friction of paying)
- **When:** you have multiple offers available; trained sales reps. "I could teach a master class on this upsell. It's made me millions." See also **The Economist play** (3 options, price the bundle = the expensive single to sell both).

### 2C. Anchor Upsell
- **Terminology:** "The only thing worse than making a $1,000 offer to a person with a $100 budget is making a $100 offer to someone with a $1,000 budget." Origin: the $16,000 suit → $2,200 suit.
- **5 steps:** (1) present the anchor (premium, 5–10x price) → (2) get "the gasp" → (3) come to the rescue ("Do you care about the designer much?") → (4) present main offer (now a great deal) → (5) ask how they want to pay.
- **Rule:** main + premium share the SAME primary features; only secondary features differ. "Don't treat the anchor like a fake or the customer will too." Some buy the premium = outsized profit.

### 2D. Rollover Upsell
- **Terminology:** "Want to just roll it forward?" Origin: Justin's gym ($50/mo off for a year instead of free months) — "the missing link in my money model."
- **Mechanics:** Credit some/all of a previous purchase toward the next (more expensive) offer. **WHO** to upsell: old/lapsed customers, your upset customers (better than a refund), competitors' upset customers, current customers. **WHAT:** more/better/new. **HOW:** all or part, up front or spread.
- **Pricing rule:** price the next offer at least **4x the rollover credit** (so even full credit = ≤25% discount). Add urgency — make it a one-time, "once-in-a-customer-lifetime" offer. Winback campaign: 200 personalized videos → $1.9M annual revenue. **Gift card play:** sell $200 gift cards for $20 (limit 2, usable only on others) → roll over later toward a $1,000+ offer.

## 3. DOWNSELL OFFERS ("whatever you offer after someone says no")
"No means no for this thing, not no for everything." **Cardinal rule: never offer the same thing for less** (that's discounting and destroys trust — "I don't negotiate with terrorists"). You CAN offer something different for less, or the same thing paid over time. **Three named downsells:**

### 3A. Payment Plan Downsell (changes HOW they pay, not total price)
- **Terminology:** "How much can you put down today?"
- **7-step ladder (stop when they buy):** (1) Reward paying in full — present price WITH interest baked in, then offer prepay discount ("$15, but $10 if you prepay"); (2) third-party financing / credit card / layaway; (3) half now, half on next paycheck; (4) **temperature check** — "scale 1–10, how bad do you want this?" (8+ keep going; 7- pivot to feature downsell); (5) split into 3 payments; (6) evenly spread payments; (7) offer a free trial (→ next play).
- **Key data:** monthly billing = 10.7% monthly churn; quarterly = 5%; annual = 2%. So "start high, fewer bigger payments, and work your way down." Align payment dates to paychecks → fewer declines. **Seesaw downselling:** "Would you rather have giant monthly payments or tiny ones?"

### 3B. Trial With Penalty (recurring products/services)
- **Terminology:** "If you do X, Y, Z, I'll let you start for free." (vs. Win-Your-Money-Back which REFUNDS if you comply; here you only PAY if you DON'T comply.)
- **5 steps:** (1) offer the trial LAST; (2) always get a card; (3) always sell "staying and paying" — "If this program got you the result, will you stay long-term?"; (4) explain fees AFTER getting the card ("this is how we've always done it"); (5) make check-ins required (= upsell opportunities).
- **Criteria:** reuse your Win-Your-Money-Back criteria. Just call it a "free trial." Use for recurring offers where the customer must do work to get results.

### 3C. Feature Downsell (changes WHAT they get, lowers price by removing features — NOT discounting)
- **Terminology:** "Why don't we try this instead?" Tripled close rate 25%→75% by removing the guarantee.
- **Mechanics:** Remove features from HIGHEST to lowest value, lower price, ask "How about now?" "People see the value in what you removed after they see the price difference" — often flips them back to the original offer. Lower quantity, quality, cheaper alternatives, or cut components (e.g. drop the guarantee).
- **Notes:** Name combos (whale package / total transformation / "the minimum"). Temperature-check after two downsells. **Alternate between payment-plan and feature downsells** — "you become very difficult to refuse." Free orientations boost DIY downsells. Feature-downsold customers have the 2nd-highest LTV. Barter discounts for reviews/testimonials/referrals.

## 4. CONTINUITY OFFERS (ongoing value for ongoing payments until cancel)
"You can shear sheep for a lifetime, but you can only skin it once." Make continuity LAST (after attraction/upsell/downsell) so you keep 30-day cash AND stack recurring revenue. "No successful continuity business I've seen has a standalone membership offer." **Three named continuity offers:**

### 4A. Continuity Bonus Offer
- **Terminology:** "If you like this, you're going to love what I have next." / "If you sign up today, you also get XYZ."
- **Mechanics:** Give an awesome bonus (worth MORE than the first payment) for signing up today. Advertise the bonus, NOT the membership. Make bonuses things you already have/do, with realistic anchor values. Anchor the bonus first, then reveal they get it free by joining.
- **Continuity-vs-standalone pricing data (% choosing continuity):** standalone 1.33x monthly → ~50%; 1.66x → 60%; 2x → 70%; 2.33x → 80%; 2.66x → 90%. "The smaller the standalone price vs continuity, the more buy standalone." Then offer bulk prepaid discount upsell.

### 4B. Continuity Discount Offer
- **Terminology:** "If you sign up today, you get X time free." Origin: the trash-business neighbor (1 free year for a 5-year contract).
- **Mechanics:** Give free time if they commit. Apply the discount 4 ways: up front (only for industries that enforce contracts), at the end (earned), spread evenly, or after the first/second payment (collects cash). Front-load = more conversions, higher churn; back-load = fewer conversions, lower churn.
- **HIGHEST-VALUE TIP IN THE BOOK:** **Bill every 4 weeks, not monthly** — 13 cycles/year vs 12 = +8.3% revenue for changing a few words (at 20% margins → +41% profit). Also add a **3% "processing fee"** (offer to waive it for a second/ACH payment method). Earn a **lifetime discount at your average churn point.** Cancellation: make the fee = the discount they received.

### 4C. Waved Fee Offer
- **Terminology:** "You can sign up month-to-month with the setup fee, or I'll wave it if you commit to a year."
- **Mechanics:** Set a startup fee = 3–5x monthly rate. Waive it for a year+ commitment; if they cancel early, they pay the fee. "Customers will stay longer if leaving costs more than staying." Larger fee → more commitments; smaller fee → more upfront cash. Best for 1-year+ commitments and slow-to-work services (SEO, investing, weight loss). Optional: donate the cancellation fee "to a cause they hate."

## EXAMPLE FULL MODELS (from the book)
- **Gym Launch:** decoy attraction ($16k DWY) → classic upsell (Gym Lords $42k/yr) → payment-plan/seesaw downsell → menu upsell + feature downsell continuity ($100–800/wk tiers). Grew to $4.4M/mo by month 20.
- **Micro gym:** Win-Your-Money-Back → payment-plan/trial-with-penalty downsell → menu upsell (supplements) → feature downsell → rollover + lifetime discount continuity.
- **Newsletter "six-headed monster":** free trial ($0 → $3.99/mo) + pay-less-now-pay-more-later + lifetime discount (pay $297 now, keep rate for life).
