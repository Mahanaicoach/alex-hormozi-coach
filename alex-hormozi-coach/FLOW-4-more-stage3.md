# FLOW 4 — Stage 3: Profitable but plateaued → More
### Source: the live-session "More" teaching + COACHING-FRAMEWORK.md

For the business that's working and profitable but stuck — "I can't grow," "I'm maxed out," "I'm supply-constrained." The answer is almost never *new*; it's *more* of what already works. This stage is also the reframe library the diagnostic loop (FLOW 3.1, step 6) pulls from.

---

## FLOW 4.1 — Constraint diagnosis (demand vs supply)
**When:** any "I'm stuck" or scaling question.
**Steps:**
1. Ask: "If I doubled your leads tomorrow, could you deliver them all?"
   - **No → supply-constrained.** The bottleneck is delivery/hiring → run 4.4 (parallel machine).
   - **Yes → demand-constrained.** The bottleneck is leads/sales → run 4.2 (volume) on the Core Four.
2. Name the single biggest drop-off point (the constraint) — "the step with the biggest bang for the buck."
3. Apply the **20% rule** before any change: a change to what's working costs a near-guaranteed ~20% output drop for uncertain upside. Only change if the upside clearly beats that. Otherwise → **do more of the same.**
**Done when:** constraint is named and you know whether to add volume (4.2) or build a machine (4.4).

---

## FLOW 4.2 — "What would it take" (the volume unlock)
**When:** demand-constrained and currently doing too little volume.
**Steps:**
1. State the big goal as if urgent: "What would it take to hit [10× goal] in 6 months?"
2. Have them describe the **actions** required at that volume (e.g. 2,000 ads, not 25; one sale/day needs 7× current advertising).
3. Ask: "What **resources** would that require?" (editors, recruiters, spend). Get the number.
4. Ask: "Is it **worth it**?" Compare resource cost to the prize. Usually a resounding yes.
5. Ask: "So **what's stopping you**?" Usually nothing.
6. Set the volume with a **Rule of 100** and hold it consistently — "volume negates luck; one day isn't enough."
**Reframes to deploy here:**
- **More > Better > New.** More is the highest risk-adjusted return — the thing already works.
- **Absolute > relative returns.** "$1M in → $2M back beats $10K in → $100K back." Stop optimizing percentages; chase total output.
- **Maximizer, not optimizer.** Diminishing returns are still returns.
**Done when:** a concrete volume target, the resources to hit it, and a start date are set.

---

## FLOW 4.3 — ICE bet selection (pick the ONE move)
**When:** they have many ideas and are scattering / over-strategizing.
**Steps:**
1. Score each candidate move on **Impact** (how big), **Confidence** (how likely), **Ease** (resources needed).
2. Pick the highest-ICE bet. "If one move could double the business, why do three?"
3. Kill the rest for now. Limited resources force prioritization — pick one or two material bets per year, not five.
**Reframe:** "Don't run your business off the fridge ('what can I whip up?'). Decide what you want to eat, then go get the ingredients."
**Done when:** one bet is chosen and the others are explicitly shelved.

---

## FLOW 4.4 — Build the parallel machine (supply side)
**When:** supply-constrained — can't hire/train/deliver fast enough.
**Core idea:** hiring is just customer acquisition. Map talent to the same funnel: **leads → nurture → sale → activation → retention → ascension** = **applications → interviews → onboarding → retain → promote.** If you can build one funnel, build the other.
**Steps:**
1. Compute the unit economics of a hire: gross profit per producer vs. cost to acquire one (recruiter fee, ads, relocation). (Corey: $200K GP/tech; Phil: $100K GP/provider.)
2. Decide how much you'll **spend to acquire a producer** — usually far more than you currently do. (Phil: spin up 10 recruiters at $25K w/ a 6-month replace guarantee.)
3. Build the recruiting **Core Four**: warm (network), cold (recruiting outreach), content (job postings), paid (Indeed/FB ads). Run to **group interviews** for efficiency (assume ~30% show; book 5× your target).
4. Fix **activation/training**: find what successful producers had in common (demographics, past experience, behaviors); force that path for everyone. **Make training approximate reality** (Travis: freestanding doors, hostile-customer role-play, "practice like you play"). Gamify with leaderboards beyond just closes.
5. Set **expectations honestly** at recruiting (the Shackleton ad: state the hard truth; it attracts the right people).
6. Add the **3-step career path**: sell → teach one person to sell → recruit a team. This unlocks referral-based hiring (your producers bring producers).
**Reframe:** **feature, not a bug** — high churn in hard roles (door-to-door) is the nature of the model; the answer is more volume + better activation, not a "fix."
**Done when:** a repeatable producer-acquisition machine exists with target volume, spend, and an activation/training path.

---

## FLOW 4.5 — Internalize the constraint (mindset reframe)
**When:** they blame an external thing ("salespeople suck," "Facebook doesn't work," "I can't find talent").
**Steps:**
1. Restate their complaint as a capability: "It's not that salespeople suck — it's that you haven't built the training to make them succeed."
2. Point the lever back at them: the constraint is almost always a skill they haven't built yet, not the world.
3. Route to the matching machine flow (4.4 for talent, FLOW-1 channels for leads).
**Done when:** the problem is reframed as buildable and assigned to a flow.

---

## Stage 3 reminders (verbatim spirit)
- "Consistency is the rarest trait." Volume must be *sustained* — back to zero every Monday.
- "You won't get credit for the work. You'll get credit for the outcome." Do it anyway.
- "What would it take → what resources → is it worth it → what's stopping you?" is the whole engine.
