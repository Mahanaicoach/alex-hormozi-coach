# REF-offers — Alex Hormozi, *$100M Offers* (Acquisition.com Vol. 1, 2021)

Coaching reference. Frameworks in book order. Each entry: terminology → components → when to prescribe → verbatim lines.

---

## 0. Core thesis & the Grand Slam Offer
- **Hormozi's terminology:** "Grand Slam Offer."
- **Definition (his words):** "An offer you present to the marketplace that cannot be compared to any other product or service available. Combining an attractive promotion, an unmatchable value proposition, a premium price, and an unbeatable guarantee with a money model (payment terms)... allows you to sell in a category of one."
- **What it does:** (1) increases response rates, (2) increases conversions, (3) lets you charge premium prices. "It gets you more customers, at higher ticket prices, for less money."
- **When to reach for it:** Any client stuck competing on price, with thin margins, or whose marketing "looks the same as everyone else's." This is the whole-book payoff — prescribe the full build (Ch. 9–15) when the offer itself is the bottleneck.
- **Verbatim:** "Make people an offer so good they would feel stupid saying no." (Travis Jones / TJ) · "Commoditized = price-driven (race to the bottom); Differentiated = value-driven (category of one)."

## 1. Three ways to grow (Ch. 3)
- **Components:** (1) Get more customers, (2) increase average purchase value, (3) get them to buy more times. Collapses to two: **get more customers** + **increase each customer's value**. "Grow or die. Maintenance is a myth."
- **When:** Diagnostic opener — locate which lever the client is failing on before designing the offer.

## 2. Finding the right market — "A Starving Crowd" (Ch. 4)
- **Four market indicators (in order):** (1) **Massive pain**, (2) **Purchasing power**, (3) **Easy to target**, (4) **Growing**.
- **Three eternal markets:** Health, Wealth, Relationships. Find a niche subgroup inside one.
- **Order of importance (3 levers):** **Market > Offer > Persuasion.** A great rating on a higher lever overpowers everything below; a "bad" market stops the equation.
- **When:** Client says "this market is bad" / can't sell no matter what. Check the four indicators before touching the offer. Also when they keep niche-hopping.
- **Verbatim:** "The pain is the pitch." · "Don't make me niche slap you... Riches are in the niches." · "Don't be romantic about your audience." · **Niche pricing:** same product, 100x the price — "Time management" $19 → "...for B2B outbound power tools & gardening sales reps" $1,997.

## 3. Pricing — charge a premium (Ch. 3, 5)
- **Logic:** Avoid the **price-to-value discrepancy** (value dipping below price = they stop buying). Don't lower price — raise value, then raise price.
- **Virtuous Cycle of Price:** Raising price → ↑ client emotional investment → ↑ perceived value → ↑ results (they're invested) → attracts best clients (cheapest to serve) → multiplies margin to reinvest. Lowering price runs the cycle in reverse ("your world sucks").
- **When:** Client undercharging, attracting bad clients, no margin to deliver well, copying competitors' prices.
- **Verbatim:** "Charge as high a price as you can say out loud without cracking a smile." (Dan Kennedy) · "There's no strategic benefit to being the second cheapest in the marketplace, but there is for being the most expensive." · "Price it so it stings a little... those who pay the most pay the most attention." · "99% of businesses need to raise their prices to grow, not lower them. Profit is oxygen."

## 4. THE VALUE EQUATION (Ch. 6) — the centerpiece
**Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)**

Four drivers — increase the top two, decrease the bottom two:
1. **Dream Outcome** (↑) — the gap between their reality and dreams; tied to *status*. "Frame benefits in terms of status gained from the viewpoint of others."
2. **Perceived Likelihood of Achievement** (↑) — certainty. "People pay for certainty." (10,000th patient vs. the surgeon's first).
3. **Time Delay** (↓) — long-term outcome vs. short-term experience. Engineer **fast early wins**: "They buy the dream, but they stay for the benefits they discover along the way."
4. **Effort & Sacrifice** (↓) — tangible + intangible cost. Done-for-you > DIY. "Fast beats free."
- **Key rule:** It's a *division* equation — "if you can make the bottom equal zero, you are golden... anything divided by zero is infinite value." Beginners over-focus the top (claims are easy/lazy); "the best companies focus on the bottom side — making things immediate, seamless, and effortless" (Apple, Amazon, Netflix).
- **Perception is reality:** value only counts once the prospect *perceives* it (London tube dotted map > faster trains). Reach for **psychological** solutions, not logical ones ("any fool can sell with a discount").
- **When:** Use as the universal lens to diagnose any weak offer and to score deliverables. Example contrast: Meditation (1.5/4) vs. Xanax (4/4) — same dream outcome, opposite scores on the other three.

## 5. Offer creation — Problems → Solutions (Ch. 8–9)
Use **divergent thinking** (many answers), warmed up via the **Brick Exercise** (120s, list every use of a brick).

**Five-step creation process:**
- **Step 1 — Identify the Dream Outcome.** Sell the destination, not the vehicle ("no one wants a membership; they want to lose weight").
- **Step 2 — List Problems.** Walk the sequence the customer experiences ("what happens immediately before and after"). Every problem maps to one of the four value drivers. "The more problems you can think of, the more problems you get to solve."
- **Step 3 — Turn Problems into Solutions.** Reverse each problem into "how to..." solution-oriented language (copywriting 101).
- **Step 4 — Create the Delivery Vehicles (the "how").** Brainstorm every possible way to solve each problem.
- **Step 5 — Trim & Stack** (see §6).
- **Rule:** "You will solve *every* problem." "Don't get romantic about how you want to solve the problem." (eating-out-guide anecdote — one unresolved objection kills sales).

## 6. Delivery Cube + Trim & Stack (Ch. 10)
- **Sales-to-Fulfillment Continuum:** easy to sell = hard to fulfill, and vice versa; find the sweet spot. Mantra: **"Create flow, monetize flow, then add friction."** Overdeliver at first, then systematize.
- **Delivery Cube (cheat codes for variation):** (A) personal attention — 1-on-1 / small group / one-to-many; (B) effort expected — DIY / done-with-you / done-for-you; (C) medium if live — in person, phone, email, text, Zoom, chat; (D) if recorded — audio/visual/written; (E) responsiveness — 24/7, 9–5, within 5 min, etc.; (F) **10x-to-1/10th test** — "If they paid me 10x (or $100k), what would I provide? How could I still make them successful for 1/10th the price?"
- **Trim & Stack (Step 5A + 5B):** Remove highest-cost/lowest-value first, then low-cost/low-value. **Keep: low-cost/high-value and high-cost/high-value.** Favor **high-value one-to-many** solutions (biggest cost-to-value gap; "this is exactly why software is so valuable"). Then **stack** the survivors into one bundled high-value deliverable that (1) solves all perceived problems, (2) gives you conviction it's one-of-a-kind, (3) is impossible to compare to competitors.
- **When:** After problems/solutions list exists and you need a scalable, profitable, decommoditized core offer.

## SECTION 4 — ENHANCING THE OFFER (Ch. 11–15)
Frame: **all marketing shifts the supply/demand curve.** "Increase demand, decrease perceived supply." **Delicate Dance of Desire** — "Desire is a contract you make with yourself to be unhappy until you get what you want" (Naval). Sell fewer units than you can. **"The longer you delay the ask, the bigger the ask you can make."** Keep prospects "ravenous, not merely aroused."

## 7. Scarcity (Ch. 11) — function of *quantity*
- **Three types:** (1) limited supply of seats/slots (general or per period), (2) limited supply of bonuses, (3) **never available again**.
- **Service scarcity tactics:** (1) **Total business cap** ("only 25 clients total"), (2) **Growth-rate cap** ("only X new clients per week"), (3) **Cohort cap** ("X clients per class/cohort"). Plus **Honest scarcity** — just publish your real capacity ("we're 81% full").
- **Rules:** "Always sell out" and "let everyone know you sold out" (social proof). Once a month is the sweet spot. Extreme scarcity = cap tiny one-on-one access at a high price. "Once you're out, you can never come back."
- **When:** Client wants faster decisions / higher prices without changing the product; especially high-ticket upsells.
- **Verbatim:** "When demand increases, cut supply." (George at the fundraiser) · "Fear of loss is stronger than desire for gain."

## 8. Urgency (Ch. 12) — function of *time*
- **Four types:** (1) **Cohort-based / rolling urgency** ("sign up today, start Monday, or wait"), (2) **Rolling seasonal urgency** (real countdown dates; rename by season), (3) **Pricing/bonus-based urgency** (the promo, not the service, expires — keeps integrity for year-round sellers), (4) **Exploding opportunity** (arbitrage that decays with time).
- **Key data point:** "The biggest sales on a week-long launch happen in the last 4 hours of the last day — up to 50–60%." Don't fear "losing" sales; non-buyers were never going to buy.
- **When:** Long sales cycles, stalled pipelines, local businesses (vary marketing more often). "Deadlines drive decisions." · Pro tip: "Clean your pipeline with every price change."

## 9. Bonuses (Ch. 13)
- **Principle:** "A single offer is less valuable than the same offer broken into component parts and stacked as bonuses." Anchor price to the core offer, then widen the price-to-value gap with each bonus. **"Add bonuses instead of discounting"** — discounting teaches customers your prices are negotiable.
- **1-on-1 selling sequence:** Ask for the sale *first*; if yes, reveal bonuses after (wow). If no, present a bonus matching their specific objection, then ask again (reciprocity).
- **Bonus bullets (11 rules):** always offer them; give a benefit-driven name; explain relation→what it is→how you created it→how it improves their life; provide proof; paint the vivid after-image; ascribe & justify a price tag; tools/checklists > more trainings (lower effort = higher value); each addresses a specific obstacle; solve their *next* problem; **the bonuses' total value should eclipse the core offer**; stack scarcity/urgency onto bonuses too.
- **Advanced:** Use **other people's products/services** as bonuses (free for you, exposure for them); negotiate group discounts + a referral commission so bonuses become revenue streams.
- **When:** Close stalls, low perceived value, or you want to raise value without cutting price.

## 10. Guarantees (Ch. 14) — reverse the risk
- **Principle:** "The single greatest objection... is risk." A guarantee can 2–4x conversion (Jason Fladlien). "Don't be emotional, just do the math" — higher sales usually outweigh higher refunds (130 sales / 13 refunds = 1.23x vs. 100 / 5).
- **Four types:**
  1. **Unconditional** — pay first, no-questions-asked refund / trial. Strongest pull; best for lower-ticket B2C. Risky for high-cost fulfillment.
  2. **Conditional** — "If you don't get X result in Y time, we will Z." Add the actions required for success as conditions. Subtypes: outsized refund (2–3x), service guarantee (keep working free until result — "my personal favorite"), modified service (extra Y period), credit-based, personal-service, hotel+airfare, wage-payment, release-of-service, delayed-second-payment, first-outcome.
  3. **Anti-guarantee** — "All sales are final." Use for exclusive/consumable items that lose value once seen; needs a strong "reason why" (damaging admission).
  4. **Implied** — performance models: revshare, profit share, ratchets, triggers, bonuses. "If I don't perform, I don't get paid." Best alignment; needs measurable outcome + trust/control on collection.
- **Rules:** Give it **teeth** ("...or we will Z" — without the "or what," it's weak). **Stack** guarantees (unconditional + conditional, or sequential conditionals). **Name it** ("Club a Baby Seal Guarantee"). High-cost fulfillment → use conditional/anti, not unconditional. Guarantees are *enhancers* — they can't fix a bad product/sales team.
- **When:** Risk is the stated or sensed objection; tie conditions to the actions that drive client success.
- **Verbatim:** "Don't be emotional. Just do the math." · Hormozi's own weight-loss guarantee took only 2 refunds on 4,000 sales in 3.5 years.

## 11. Naming — the MAGIC formula (Ch. 15)
- **M-A-G-I-C:** **M**agnet (reason why/promotion: free, % off, season, grand opening) · **A**vatar (who it's for; go hyper-local) · **G**oal (dream outcome) · **I**nterval (time duration — careful with platform compliance on quantifiable claims) · **C**ontainer (bundle word: challenge, blueprint, bootcamp, intensive, accelerator, system, etc.).
- **Rules:** Use **3–5** of the 5 (all is usually too long); order can flex; shorter/punchier wins. Bonus techniques: **rhyme** and **alliteration** ("Six-Pack Fast Track," "Big Booty Boot Camp"). Name sub-items/bonuses too.
- **Why it matters:** Offers **fatigue** (faster in local markets). You change the *wrapper*, not the offer.
- **Offer fatigue fix order (lightest → heaviest):** (1) creative/images, (2) body copy, (3) headline/wrapper name, (4) duration, (5) the free/discount enhancer, (6) the entire monetization structure (last resort).
- **When:** Ads underperforming, lead flow drying up, or launching a new offer to market.
- **Verbatim:** "We are not changing the actual offer. We are only changing the wrapping paper." · "People do judge a book by its cover."
