# REF-leads — $100M Leads Framework Reference

Source: Alex Hormozi, *$100M Leads: How to Get Strangers to Want to Buy Your Stuff*. This book is the playbook for clients who are JUST STARTING (zero/low revenue, no audience, no ad budget). It sits on top of *$100M Offers* (assume the client already has a Grand Slam Offer). The whole book moves from "zero clients / zero money / zero skills" to "$100M leads machine." Core thesis: **"You're not getting as many leads as you want because you're not advertising enough. Period."** Advertising = "the process of making known." All growth comes from getting (1) more, (2) better, (3) cheaper, (4) reliable leads.

---

## 1. Lead vs. Engaged Lead (the foundational definitions)
- **Lead** = "a person you can contact." That's it (phone contacts, emails, followers, a door you knock on — all leads).
- **Engaged Lead** = "people who *show* interest in the stuff you sell" (opt in, reply, follow + contactable, book a call). **"Engaged leads are the true output of advertising."**
- **When to use:** the very first reframe with any new client. Stop them chasing vanity "leads"; the job is producing *engaged* leads.
- Rule of thumb: "If you can contact them, they're leads." / The point of all advertising is engaged leads.

## 2. Lead Magnet (engaging leads before they buy)
- **Definition:** "A complete solution to a narrow problem" — a low-cost or free offer that solves one narrow problem and reveals the next problem, which your **core offer** solves (for money). Analogy: salty pretzels at a bar → makes you thirsty → buy a drink.
- **Core offer first:** "Advertising your core offer might be all you need... try this way first." Use a lead magnet when the product is more expensive / needs explanation.
- **Seven Steps to Create an Effective Lead Magnet:**
  1. Figure out the problem to solve & who to solve it for (use the **Problem-Solution Cycle** — every solution reveals a new problem; pick a narrow, meaningful one your core offer can then solve).
  2. Figure out how to solve it — **3 types of lead magnets:** (a) **Reveal their problem** (diagnosis: speed test, posture analysis, termite inspection), (b) **Samples & trials** (free/brief access to the core offer — Costco samples), (c) **One step of a multi-step process** (give one valuable step free).
  3. Figure out how to deliver it — **4 delivery methods:** software, information, services, physical products. (3 types × 4 methods = up to 12 versions; rotate them.)
  4. Test what to name it — headline > image > sub-headline, in that order. "When you have written your headline you have spent 80 cents of your advertising dollar" (Ogilvy). Run polls/A1-vs-A2.
  5. Make it easy to consume — offer every format (video, text, audio, etc.); 2–4x take rates.
  6. Make it darn good — **"Give away the secret, sell the implementation."** "Make your lead magnets as good as your paid stuff."
  7. Make it easy to tell you they want more — a **CTA** with (1) what to do + (2) reason to act now (scarcity, urgency, "make up a reason / because…").
- **When to use:** client has an expensive offer, weak opt-in rates, or high cost-per-call. Lead magnet math: a $25 free magnet can cut cost-per-customer ~3x vs. advertising the core offer directly.
- Rule of thumb: "Make your lead magnet so good people feel stupid saying no." / "A person who pays with their time now is more likely to pay with their money later."

## 3. THE CORE FOUR (the only 4 ways one person can advertise)
Two audiences (**warm** = gave permission to contact / **cold** = strangers) × two modes (**one-to-one / private** vs **one-to-many / public**) = the Core Four:
- **One-to-one + warm = Warm Outreach**
- **One-to-many + warm = Post Content**
- **One-to-one + cold = Cold Outreach**
- **One-to-many + cold = Paid Ads**

"If you aren't getting as many leads as you want, you're not doing the Core Four with enough skill or volume." Build order for a beginner: warm outreach → post content → cold outreach → paid ads. **You only need to do ONE to eat — pick one and max it out.** Trade either time or money: more time than money → content; more money than time → cold outreach or paid ads.

### 3A. WARM OUTREACH (start here — cheapest, most reliable, gets your FIRST FIVE CLIENTS)
- One-to-one to people who know you. "The cheapest and easiest way to find people interested in your stuff, and most businesses don't do it."
- **10 steps:** (1) Get your list (everyone has one — phone, email, all social platforms; "this will be your first thousand leads"). (2) Pick the platform with the most contacts. (3) Personalize the greeting (use something you know about them). (4) Reach out to **100 people/day**, up to 3 times (once/day for 3 days, or once/week by mail) until they respond. (5) Warm them up with the **ACA framework: Acknowledge, Compliment, Ask** another question. (6) Invite their friends / make an offer. (7) **Make them the easiest offer in the world — FREE** (see Your First Five Clients). (8) Start at the top (exhaust one platform, move to the next). (9) Start charging once referrals come (the "+5 rule" below). (10) Keep your list warm with regular value + Dean Jackson's "**Are you still looking to ___?**" 9-word email.
- **When to use:** brand-new business/product, no audience, no money; or re-engaging a list / launching a new product line.
- **Benchmarks:** ~1 in 5 contacts engage (100 reachouts → ~20 replies → ~4 take the free offer → ~1 paying customer). "This process alone can take you to $100,000 per year."
- Rule of thumb: "Yeses give me opportunity, nos give me feedback — either way I win." / Daily card: 100 attempts, first 4 hours of your day.

### 3B. YOUR FIRST FIVE CLIENTS / START FREE → FLIP TO PAID (sub-framework inside warm outreach)
- **"Make the first five free."** Why: (1) get reps / calm nerves making offers, (2) you probably suck now and people forgive free, (3) you learn to suck less by doing more, (4) free customers convert to paying, refer, and give testimonials.
- Free script frame: *"Since I'm only taking on five people, I can give you all the attention you need to get brag-worthy results — for free, so long as you (1) use it, (2) give me feedback, (3) leave a killer review if it deserves one. Sound fair?"*
- **The litmus test to start charging:** "Once people start referring, start charging." Then the **"+5 / raise every five" rule:** free → 80% off for next 5 → 60% off → 40% off → keep raising 20% every 5 until you hit your sweet spot. (Also creates real urgency: prices actually go up.)
- **Prepay + Guarantee twist ("Dr. Cashey"):** "Would you rather pay less today or get all your money back?" — pay-less = payment plan / no guarantee; get-all-back = prepay + guarantee. Most choose prepay-with-guarantee → more cash up front.
- **When to use:** the literal first move for a zero-revenue client; also for any brand-new product line.

### 3C. THE VALUE EQUATION (used to construct every offer/ask — carried over from $100M Offers)
Four elements: (1) **Dream Outcome**, (2) **Perceived Likelihood of Achievement**, (3) **Time Delay**, (4) **Effort & Sacrifice**. Maximize the first two, minimize the last two. Backwards-fill template: *"I help [ideal customer] get [dream outcome] in [time] without [effort/sacrifice]."*

### 3D. THE 11 WAYS TO SHOW PROOF / INCREASE PERCEIVED LIKELIHOOD OF ACHIEVEMENT
(Listed explicitly as a "pro tip: 11 ways to increase perceived likelihood of achievement." Include one or more in any offer.)
1. Show proof you've done it (your own story).
2. Proof of people just like them getting it (testimonials).
3. Sheer volume of happy reviews (lots of 5-stars; or # of people helped).
4. Certifications / degrees / third-party accreditations.
5. Numbers, stats, research supporting the outcome.
6. Experts vouching for you.
7. A new/unique characteristic they haven't failed with before.
8. Celebrities endorsing you.
9. Guarantee (skin in the game).
10. How well you describe them / their current pain ("he really gets me").
11. Demonstrate the outcome live or via recording.
- **When to use:** any time close rates lag because the prospect doesn't believe it'll work for them.

### 3E. POST FREE CONTENT (build a warm audience one-to-many)
- "The content you create isn't the compounding asset — the **audience** is." Volume works: "You just gotta do more, bro." When he 10x'd content, audience grew 10x.
- **The Content Unit = Hook, Retain, Reward:**
  - **Hook** (get attention): pick interesting **Topics** (5 buckets: far past, recent past, present, trending, manufactured); **Headlines** (news components — recency, relevancy, celebrity, proximity, conflict, unusual, ongoing); **Format** to match the platform.
  - **Retain** (drive curiosity / embed questions): **Lists, Steps, Stories.**
  - **Reward** (value per second; "there's no such thing as too long, only too boring"). "The audience decides" — if your audience grows, it's good.
- Short vs. long content = linking more content units. **Make all content for strangers** (so it gets shared).
- **Monetize — the Give:Ask ratio → "Give Give Give Give until they Ask":** give in public, ask in private; let the audience self-select when ready to buy. (TV ~3.5:1, Facebook ~4:1; growing platforms overgive massively.) Two ask styles: **integrated** (CTA inside content) and **intermittent** (e.g., 10 give posts, 11th asks). Ask for either the lead magnet (lower risk) or core offer.
- **Scaling:** *Depth-then-Width* (max one platform, then add) vs. *Width-then-Depth* (get on all early, then max together). Pro tip: only ONE CTA at a time ("a confused mind doesn't buy").
- **7 content lessons:** (1) switch "how to" → "how I"; (2) "we need to be reminded more than taught" (repeat yourself); (3) puddles→ponds→lakes→oceans (narrow first); (4) content creates sales tools; (5) free content retains paying customers; (6) "people don't have shorter attention spans, they have higher standards"; (7) avoid pre-scheduling posts.
- **When to use:** client has more time than money, wants compounding/inbound leads, and to make all other advertising convert better (78% of his clients consumed 2+ pieces of content before booking).
- Rule of thumb: "Give until they ask." / "The best day to start posting was the day you were born; the second best is today."

### 3F. COLD OUTREACH (one-to-one to strangers — for speed/predictability)
- For when you want to grow faster or want more predictable lead flow. The "advanced cousin of warm outreach." Strangers create 3 problems: no way to contact → ignore you → not interested. Solve in order: (1) get a way to contact, (2) figure out what to say, (3) contact until ready.
- **What to say: Personalize + Big Fast Value.** Make the cold message look warm (1–3 personal facts + compliment). **"Give away the farm"** — give big fast value in under 30 seconds; give stuff people would actually pay for (his take rates 3x'd when he gave free service instead of a "game-planning call"). Pro tip: rewriting the template below a 3rd-grade reading level got +50% responses.
- **Volume (3 levers):** (a) automate delivery, (b) automate distribution (sacrifice some personalization for scale; more personalization for small/high-value lists), (c) follow up more times in more ways (email first; then call "to follow up about my email"; after exhausting the list, wait 3–6 months and run it again — circumstances change).
- **Benchmarks / "Count in 100s":** ~3% of a list becomes engaged leads via email; phone ~20% pickup × 25% take. "You're doing well when LTGP is at least 3x your CAC." Don't set a daily goal below 100; don't stop for 100 days.
- **7 benefits:** one perfect message (no ad fatigue); competitors can't see it; reliable/clockwork; few platform changes; less compliance pain; no-spokesperson = sellable business; hard to copy.
- **Low-ticket note:** if cold outreach can't be profitable for a low-ticket D2C product, use it to recruit **affiliates** instead.
- **When to use:** client has more money than time, a higher-ticket offer, and wants predictable, private, competitor-proof lead flow.

### 3G. PAID ADS (one-to-many to strangers — fastest scale)
- "The fastest way to get the most people to see your stuff." A game of **efficiency**, not reach. Narrow from "the whole world" to the right needles: pick platform → target → craft ad that repels everyone else → CTA.
- **4 problems → 4 steps:** (1) **Where** (pick a platform meeting 4 criteria; "place ads where competitors place ads"). (2) **Target** (lookalike audiences from your customer list + filters: age, income, interest, location). (3) **Make the ad** = **Call-Out + Value + CTA**:
  - **Call-Out** (most important; "harness the cocktail party effect"): verbal (labels, yes-questions, if-then statements, ridiculous results) + non-verbal (contrast, likeness, the scene). Pro tip: 30+ call-outs × 10 ads = 300 variations.
  - **Value — the What/Who/When framework:** *What* = the 8 elements (4 value drivers + 4 opposites/nightmares); *Who* = status change across the people in their life (spouse, kids, boss, rivals); *When* = past/present/future timeline.
  - **CTA:** spell out exactly what to do; add urgency/scarcity/bonuses.
  - (4) **Get permission to contact** = simple landing page that matches the ad.
- **Money / scaling (3 phases):** Phase 1 **Track money** (set up tracking first). Phase 2 **Lose money** ("investing in a money-printing machine"; budget 2x the 30-day cash collected per customer to test an ad; expect to lose more often than win, but win big and 10x down on winners). Phase 3 **Print money** (reverse the budget from your customer goal; pad 20%).
- **Efficiency metric: LTGP : CAC** (Lifetime Gross Profit ÷ Cost to Acquire a Customer). **Need ≥ 3:1.** Diagnose: if CAC is below 3x industry avg → fix the business model (raise LTGP); if above → fix the ads.
- **Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA):** make back the cost to get AND fulfill a customer within the first 30 days (e.g., via an upsell), so a credit card funds infinite scale. "The key to limitless scale."
- **When to use:** client has money to risk, tracking in place, and a model where LTGP comfortably exceeds CAC (ideally CFA-capable). Lesson: "Don't confuse sales problems with advertising problems."

## 4. CORE FOUR ON STEROIDS — "More, Better, New"
When already doing the Core Four but want more leads:
- **More:** the **Rule of 100** — do 100 primary actions/day for 100 days straight (100 reachouts, 100 minutes of content, 100 cold reachouts, or 100 minutes making paid ads). "Top 0.1% of advertisers test 11x more creative."
- **Better:** test ONE thing per week per platform; fix the **constraint** (the step with the biggest drop-off — "biggest bang for the buck"); log every test.
- **New:** order = new placement → new platform → new Core Four activity. Beware the **"Size of the Pie Fallacy"** (assuming the tiny slice you advertise to is the whole market).
- **Order:** under $1M/yr profit → **More** first (not enough volume for % gains to matter). Over $1M → flip to **Better, More, New.**

## 5. THE FOUR LEAD GETTERS / LEAD ENHANCERS (get OTHER people to do the Core Four for you = leverage)
Lead getters aren't things you *do* — they're people you get (via the Core Four) who then advertise for you. "Leverage = how much you get for the time you spend." Four flavors, in natural progression:

### 5A. CUSTOMER REFERRALS / WORD OF MOUTH (lowest cost, highest profit, exponential)
- "The best advertising is a happy customer." Referrals are worth more (higher LTGP) and cost less (free). **Referral Growth Equation:** referrals in − churned customers out; if referrals > churn you grow with zero other advertising.
- Two reasons businesses don't get referrals: **(1) the product isn't good enough** ("if your product were exceptional, people would already know"), **(2) they don't ask.**
- **Goodwill = Value − Price.** **6 ways to give more value (→ more referrals)**, mapped to the offer: (1) better call-outs (sell better customers), (2) set better expectations (slowly lower promises to over-deliver), (3) increase likelihood of achievement (find what your best customers did, force everyone to do it, tie it to your guarantee), (4) decrease time delay (faster/more frequent wins; first 48 hrs; "deliver early, never late"), (5) decrease effort & sacrifice (keep improving the product monthly), (6) CTA — tell them what to buy next.
- **"One question to rule them all":** if you could only get customers from ONE existing customer's referrals forever, how would you treat them?
- **7 ways to ASK for referrals:** (1) one-sided benefit, (2) two-sided benefit (Dropbox/PayPal — Dropbox 39x'd in 15 months), (3) ask right at point of sale ("who else could you do this with?"), (4) referrals as a negotiation chip, (5) referral events, (6) ongoing referral programs, (7) unlockable referral bonuses. Pro tip: ask the moment they buy (gets +25–30% signups); run your paid ads "for free" off referral cash.
- **When to use:** client has product-market fit and wants compounding, near-free growth; aim for 25%+ of customers from referrals (this is Level 4 of the roadmap).

### 5B. EMPLOYEES ("the most valuable chapter in the book")
- Employees do the Core Four for you. Trade ~40 hours of doing for ~4 hours of managing. Why they make you wealthy: a business that runs without you becomes a **sellable asset** ($2M profit that runs without you ≈ $10M of value).
- Belief shift: "If I can do it, someone else can do it better. Everyone is replaceable, especially me."
- **Get employees with the INTERNAL Core Four** (same as customers): warm outreach = ask your network; cold outreach = recruiting; content = job postings; paid ads = promoting postings; + referrals, agencies (staffing firms).
- **Training = the 3 D's: Document → Demonstrate → Duplicate** (write the checklist, do it in front of them, have them do it). Train for *ability to follow directions* over results ("if they get it wrong, we made it confusing"). Give short windows to prove themselves; one piece of feedback at a time; reward the good.
- **Returns:** total payroll ÷ total engaged leads = cost per engaged lead; same LTGP:CAC ≥ 3:1 test.
- **When to use:** the client has maxed their personal advertising capacity and has money to reinvest (Level 3). "It doesn't take a genius to advertise — it's about guts, not brains. Hire and train anyone willing; train hard; promote winners."

### 5C. AGENCIES (rent skills, then take them in-house)
- Agencies are "lead-getting service businesses." Hormozi's reframe: don't depend on them — **use them to LEARN new methods and new platforms.** The standard agency model fails you (best rep gets swapped to the newest client).
- **His script:** "I want to do what you do. Work with me ~6 months, charge extra to explain *why* you make each decision, then I'll train my team and we'll move to lower-cost consulting. Are you opposed to this?"
- **10-point checklist to pick a good agency:** word-of-mouth reputation; prominent clients; a waiting list; clear sales process with realistic expectations; long-term strategy (no hacks); clear about what they need from you; regular meeting cadence; simple tracking of cost vs results; a strong offer (Value Equation); and **they're expensive** ("all good agencies are expensive, but not all expensive ones are good").
- **When to use:** client has money and wants to acquire a durable, transferable skill on a new platform fast — without years of trial and error.

### 5D. AFFILIATES & PARTNERS (highest leverage — an army that sells for you)
- An affiliate = an independent business that sells your stuff to *their* audience for money/free stuff. Offer them "a fast, simple, easy way to make commissions," not the product itself. Compounding (Allen scaled to $1.7M/mo via super-affiliates; Prestige Labs + Allen did $75M+ from 5,000+ affiliates).
- **Build an affiliate army in 6 steps:** (1) Find ideal affiliates ("who's got my leads?" — make a hit list by category). (2) Make them an offer (call-out + value: make more money from current customers, low effort, fast). (3) Qualify them (get them invested — make them a **customer** and/or an **expert/certified**; charge 10–20% of what an avg affiliate earns in year one). (4) Figure out what to pay (pay against max allowable CAC; **3-tier payout: agree → activate → sustain**, which lowers blended payout and raises LTGP:CAC; pay with product where possible — "sell 3, get it free"). (5) Get them advertising via **launches — the Whisper → Tease → Shout method** (whisper = curiosity, tease = value elements, shout = CTA; like a movie trailer rollout). (6) Keep them advertising via **integration** — 3 levels: (i) give your lead magnet away with their purchase, (ii) sell your lead magnet, (iii) sell your core offer directly. (White-label lead magnets; "employees are leads" — companies that hire a lot make great affiliates.)
- **Returns:** compare cost-to-get-an-affiliate against the gross profit of ALL customers they bring (target well above 3:1). Fixes if low: lower CAC, improve launch (activation), improve integration.
- **When to use:** advanced client who can manage people *outside* their business (Level 5+); low-ticket D2C products that need to be aggregated into high-ticket affiliate deals.

## 6. ADVERTISING IN REAL LIFE — Rule of 100, Open to Goal, One-Page Plan
- **Volume negates luck.** Story: 300 flyers failed because the mentor tests with 5,000/day. "The right action in the wrong amount still fails." "You input advertising effort; your output is engaged leads."
- **Rule of 100** (commit to inputs) vs. **Open to Goal** (work until you hit a specific *outcome*, no matter what — e.g., 5 signups/day however long it takes). His habit stack: wake ~4–5am, get straight to work, no meetings till noon.
- **One-Page Advertising Plan (5 steps):** (1) pick the lead type (customers / affiliates / employees / agencies); (2) pick Rule of 100 or Open to Goal; (3) fill the advertising checklist (Who / What / Where / To Whom / When / Why / How / How much / How many / How long); (4) do it daily until you can pay someone else to; (5) then make employees your new target and repeat → scale.

## 7. THE ROADMAP — Zero to $100M (the progression to prescribe by stage)
- **Level 1:** Your friends know about your stuff. → primary action: **warm outreach.**
- **Level 2:** You consistently let everyone you know know. → **warm outreach + post content** (he wishes he'd started with content).
- **Level 3:** You hire **employees** to do more advertising.
- **Level 4:** Product good enough for consistent **referrals** (target 25%+); fix product, build referral program.
- **Level 5:** Advertise in more places/ways with more people; ≥2 methods on multiple platforms; add **affiliates.**
- **Level 6:** Hire **killers** — experienced executives/department heads ("hire experience, not potential").
- **Level 7:** ("I'll edit once I cross a billion.")
- Timeline reality: building the full machine takes **5–10 years even when you know what to do.**

## 8. Closing parable / mindset (verbatim hooks the coach can quote)
- **"The Many-Sided Die":** every die with a green side hits its green streak if rolled enough; you can't see how many sides you (or others) have. **"You cannot lose if you do not quit."**
- "Advertising always works — it's only a matter of efficiency."
- "Do more than they do and you will have more than they have."
- "I am compensated tomorrow for the value I provide today."
