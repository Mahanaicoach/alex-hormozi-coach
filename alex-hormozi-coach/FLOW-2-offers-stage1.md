# FLOW 2 — Stage 1: Has an offer, shaky revenue → build a Grand Slam Offer
### Source: $100M Offers (see REF-offers.md)

For the person who's made a few sales but can't sell reliably, competes on price, or "looks like everyone else." Goal: an offer "so good they feel stupid saying no" — a category of one. Then pick one channel (FLOW 1.5–1.9) and run volume.

Run the master build (2.1) which calls the sub-flows in order: market → price intent → value equation → problems/solutions → delivery cube → trim & stack → scarcity → urgency → bonuses → guarantee → name.

---

## FLOW 2.1 — Master offer build (the spine)
**When:** the offer itself is the bottleneck.
**Steps:**
1. Confirm the **market** is good (run 2.2). If the market fails the 4 indicators, fix that first — "a bad market stops the equation."
2. Set **premium-price intent** (run 2.3). Decide now you're raising value to raise price, not discounting.
3. Run the **Value Equation** as the lens (2.4) — you'll score every piece against it.
4. **List problems → convert to solutions** (2.5).
5. **Delivery Cube** the solutions (2.6).
6. **Trim & Stack** into one bundled core offer (2.7).
7. Enhance: **Scarcity (2.8) → Urgency (2.9) → Bonuses (2.10) → Guarantee (2.11).**
8. **Name it** with MAGIC (2.12).
**Done when:** one named, stacked, guaranteed offer exists that can't be price-compared to a competitor.

---

## FLOW 2.2 — Validate the market (Starving Crowd)
**Steps:**
1. Score the market on the **4 indicators in order**: massive pain · purchasing power · easy to target · growing. One question each.
2. If weak, narrow into a niche inside Health / Wealth / Relationships.
3. Remember the lever order: **Market > Offer > Persuasion.** Don't out-copywrite a bad market.
**Decision branch:** market fails → stop and re-niche before building anything. ("Don't be romantic about your audience.")
**Done when:** market passes all four.

---

## FLOW 2.3 — Set premium pricing intent
**Steps:**
1. Diagnose the **price-to-value discrepancy**: are they undercharging and attracting bad clients?
2. Explain the **Virtuous Cycle of Price**: higher price → more client investment → better results → best clients → margin to reinvest.
3. Commit to "charge as high a price as you can say out loud without cracking a smile."
**Done when:** they've accepted that the fix is raise-value-then-raise-price, not discount. (Actual number comes after the build.)

---

## FLOW 2.4 — Run the Value Equation (the lens)
**Formula:** Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice).
**Steps:**
1. Score the current offer 1–4 on each of the four drivers.
2. **Raise the top two:** sharpen the dream outcome (frame as status); raise certainty (proof, mechanism, track record).
3. **Drive the bottom two toward zero:** kill time delay (engineer fast early wins); kill effort (move toward done-for-you). "Anything ÷ near-zero = near-infinite value."
4. Note: beginners over-pump the top (easy claims); the money is in crushing the bottom (immediate, seamless, effortless).
**Done when:** you know which of the four drivers is the weakest and have a move for each.

---

## FLOW 2.5 — Problems → Solutions
**Steps:**
1. State the **dream outcome** (sell the destination, not the vehicle).
2. **List every problem** the customer hits, walking their sequence ("what happens right before and right after"). Push for many — "more problems = more to solve."
3. Tag each problem to one of the four value drivers.
4. **Reverse each problem into a "how to…" solution.**
**Rule:** you will solve *every* problem — one unhandled objection kills the sale.
**Done when:** every listed problem has a paired solution.

---

## FLOW 2.6 — Delivery Cube (invent the "how")
**Steps:** for each solution, brainstorm delivery variants across: personal attention (1-on-1 / group / one-to-many) · effort (DIY / DWY / DFY) · medium (in-person, phone, Zoom, text…) · format if recorded · responsiveness. Apply the **10x / 1/10th test**: "If they paid 10×, what would I add? How could I still get them the result for 1/10th the cost?"
**Done when:** each solution has several possible delivery vehicles to choose from.

---

## FLOW 2.7 — Trim & Stack
**Steps:**
1. **Trim:** remove highest-cost/lowest-value items first, then low-cost/low-value.
2. **Keep:** low-cost/high-value and high-cost/high-value. Favor **high-value one-to-many** (best cost-to-value gap).
3. **Stack** survivors into one bundle that (a) solves all perceived problems, (b) feels one-of-a-kind, (c) can't be compared to competitors.
**Done when:** a single bundled core offer exists. Mantra: "create flow, monetize flow, then add friction."

---

## FLOW 2.8 — Scarcity (quantity)
**When:** want faster decisions / higher price without changing the product.
**Steps:** pick a cap type — total business cap ("only 25 clients"), growth-rate cap ("X new/week"), or cohort cap ("X per class"). Or use **honest scarcity** (publish real capacity: "81% full"). Always sell out; always announce you sold out.
**Done when:** a real, stated limit is in the offer.

---

## FLOW 2.9 — Urgency (time)
**When:** stalled pipelines, long cycles.
**Steps:** pick a type — rolling cohort ("start Monday or wait"), rolling seasonal (real dates), pricing/bonus expiry (the promo expires, not the service), or exploding opportunity. Remember: most launch sales land in the **last 4 hours**, so the deadline must be real.
**Done when:** a genuine deadline exists.

---

## FLOW 2.10 — Bonuses (widen the value gap)
**Steps:**
1. Break the offer into parts and **stack them as named bonuses** instead of discounting.
2. For each bonus, apply the bullet rules: benefit-driven name → relation → what it is → how you made it → how it improves their life → proof → vivid after-image → justified price tag.
3. Make the **bonuses' total value eclipse the core offer.** Tools/checklists beat more trainings (less effort).
4. Advanced: use **other people's products** as bonuses (free for you).
5. 1-on-1 sequence: ask for the sale first; reveal bonuses after a yes; if no, present the bonus that kills their specific objection, then re-ask.
**Done when:** the stack's perceived value clearly exceeds the price.

---

## FLOW 2.11 — Guarantee (reverse the risk)
**Steps:**
1. Name the biggest risk/objection the prospect feels.
2. Pick a type:
   - **Unconditional** (no-questions refund) — best low-ticket B2C; risky if fulfillment is costly.
   - **Conditional** ("if not X by Y, we Z") — tie conditions to the actions that drive success; service guarantee (keep working free) is his favorite.
   - **Anti-guarantee** ("all sales final") — for exclusive/consumable; needs a strong reason-why.
   - **Implied / performance** (revshare, ratchets) — best alignment; needs measurable outcome + collection control.
3. Give it **teeth** ("…or we will Z"). **Stack** guarantees. **Name** it.
4. "Don't be emotional, do the math" — more sales usually beat more refunds.
**Done when:** a named, teeth-bearing guarantee is attached, sized to fulfillment cost.

---

## FLOW 2.12 — Name it (MAGIC)
**Steps:** build the name from **3–5 of**: **M**agnet (reason/promo) · **A**vatar (who, hyper-local) · **G**oal (dream outcome) · **I**nterval (time) · **C**ontainer (challenge, blueprint, bootcamp, accelerator…). Use rhyme/alliteration; keep it punchy.
**Offer-fatigue fix order** (when an existing offer dies, change lightest first): creative → body copy → headline/name → duration → the free/discount enhancer → full monetization (last resort). "Change the wrapping paper, not the offer."
**Done when:** the offer has a punchy name and you know how to refresh it when it fatigues.

---

## Stage 1 graduation
Once the offer sells reliably and revenue is predictable, the constraint moves to monetization/cash flow (→ FLOW-3) or scale (→ FLOW-4).
